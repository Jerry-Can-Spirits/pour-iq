# Pour IQ — Voice and Tone

This document is binding for all customer-facing words: the marketing site, the portal, email, and anything else a venue reads. It was derived from the copy already shipped on pour-iq.co.uk and in the portal, so it describes the voice the product actually has. `CLAUDE.md` carries the highest-priority copy rules (byte-exact implementation of provided copy, claim verification, legal-date coupling); nothing here overrides them.

This document describes the voice for **evaluating and implementing** copy; it does not authorise drafting copy autonomously. Copy is decided in conversation and implemented byte-exact, per `CLAUDE.md`.

Pour IQ shares a discipline spine with Jerry Can Spirits (`docs/VOICE.md` in the `jerry-can-spirits` repo): no hype, no filler, one CTA, write to one person. But Pour IQ is software speaking to a working venue, not a spirits brand speaking to a drinker. Do not write Pour IQ copy in the JCS register or vice versa.

---

## The voice

Plainspoken. Operator-native. Honest to a fault. Calm.

Built behind a bar, not in a boardroom — and it sounds like it. The copy speaks from behind the bar, grounds every claim in a worked figure, and sells softly: "If it is not for you, you have lost twenty minutes."

The company is one accountable person, not a corporate "we". "When you email, you get me." "Your problem lands with the person who built the thing." Never write copy that implies a sales team, account managers, or support tiers. Where "we" appears, it is explicitly the founders: "We're the founders. We built it, we'll fix it."

---

## Hard rules

- No exclamation marks. Anywhere. Enthusiasm is never punctuated.
- No hype language: revolutionary, seamless, effortless, game-changing, unlock, supercharge, best-in-class, or similar. "AI" is functional, never a boast.
- Sentence case in headings, never Title Case. Headings end in a full stop: "Straight answers." "Coming, not claimed."
- One CTA per piece of content.
- Write to one person: the owner or manager of one venue.
- No manufactured urgency, no fake scarcity.
- Counts and durations in words in prose ("twelve years", "ninety days", "forty of those a week"); money and percentages as figures (£8.50, £915, 4%), set in mono even mid-sentence — "Forty of those a week is £915 a year" carries both halves of the rule in one line. Large catalogue counts may stay numerals ("around 550 ingredients").
- Short declaratives are the rhythm hammer; longer sentences build on triplets ("reads every line, logs every price, and flags what moved").

## The claims discipline

This is the load-bearing rule of the brand:

- **Only sell what is built.** Shipped features are stated plainly; everything else lives under "Coming, not claimed." Never move a roadmap item into shipped copy without verifying against the portal code.
- **Never claim cross-venue price benchmarking.** No feature, screen, or report anywhere in Pour IQ shows one venue's prices to another venue, or to us. The FAQ, privacy policy, and terms all state this; copy must never contradict it. BENCHMARK exists only as a roadmap item, anonymous in both directions, framed as future.
- **Publish unflattering numbers.** Proof pages use real figures from real venues. "If a number is unflattering, it is published anyway. That is the point of this page."
- **A tool, not an adviser.** Pour IQ never gives tax, legal, or accounting advice, never guarantees savings, and "will never quietly invent a price."

## Terminology

Use the trade's words without explaining them: GP and GP%, pour cost, serve, spec and spec cards, variance, stocktake, margin. Two branded concepts get consistent use:

- **The leak** — the core metaphor for hidden margin loss. "Your margin is leaking. Pour IQ shows you exactly where." It shows up as pennies, on every serve, every night.
- **The ripple** — one supplier price rise rippling through every recipe and serve it touches.

The customer is a **venue** (or a bar, or "you"); never "operator", never "user" in customer-facing copy. The product is **Pour IQ** on the marketing site and **Pour IQ™** in the portal, always the subject of active verbs: Pour IQ scans, reads, flags, shows. Its one-line descriptor: "Menu and cost engineering for independent UK bars." The parent company is Jerry Can Spirits. The underlying AI is Claude (Anthropic in legal copy).

Integrations are named plainly — Square, Zettle (by PayPal), SumUp in; Xero, QuickBooks out — and summarised as "Sales in… bills out. No retyping."

## Two registers, one voice

**Marketing site** — measured and persuasive. Largely avoids contractions ("it is not", "cannot see", "you will see") for a considered feel. Builds an argument: problem, fix, proof, always with worked figures ("Forty of those a week is £915 a year."). Rich in the house metaphors.

**Portal microcopy** — warm and instructional. Uses contractions freely ("you'll", "don't", "we're"). Imperative and task-focused: "Drop the file." "Read it. Don't act on anything yet." "Try one feature per week." Empty states are calm, never alarming: "Nothing needs attention right now." Literal over metaphorical: "Theoretical versus actual usage, surfaced as ml, percent, and pounds."

Shared invariants across both: sentence-case headings with full stops, zero exclamation marks, founder accountability, shipped-versus-roadmap honesty, and the standing guarantee that supplier prices are never exposed cross-venue.

## Example lines that carry the voice

From the shipped copy. When in doubt, write like these:

- "Your margin is leaking. Pour IQ shows you exactly where."
- "You know your GP on paper. The bar pours something else."
- "Built behind a bar, not in a boardroom."
- "One price. No contract. Nothing hidden."
- "Coming, not claimed."
- "If it is not for you, you have lost twenty minutes."
- "Built by the person who answers the phone."
- "Real figures from a real bar, not a case study written by marketing."
- "That is exactly why the product was built so we cannot see your buying."
- "It will never quietly invent a price."
- "We're the founders. We built it, we'll fix it."
